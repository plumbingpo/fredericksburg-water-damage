Simple Ways to Keep a Richmond Home Dry

Houses tell you when water is getting where it should not. Our technicians see the same early clues again and again in Richmond, and they are worth learning. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

None of these is an emergency on its own, but each is cheaper to fix now than later. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. If you have a sump pump, pour a bucket of water into the pit a few times a year to confirm it switches on. Keep stored items off the basement or garage floor on shelves or in plastic bins.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. What it does is make them rarer and smaller, and that matters when the alternative is days of drying equipment in your Richmond living room.

Restoration Challenges Richmond Homeowners Face

Most water damage calls we take in Richmond trace back to a short list of causes. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Lines in exterior walls, garages and unheated attics are the usual trouble spots, depending on the season. Corroded fittings and worn supply lines can also let go without any warning in older homes. Shut off the main valve right away, because an open line can soak several rooms quickly.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. A water heater near the end of its life can leak from the tank bottom or fail all at once. We see many of these in Richmond homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: It takes only a few minutes for a blocked toilet or a forgotten faucet to flood a bathroom. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. Water that backed up out of a drain or toilet bowl is not clean, so keep kids and pets out of it.

Roof: Rain gets in through worn flashing, damaged shingles or gutters that have stopped draining. By the time a brown ring appears overhead, the attic insulation has often been wet for a while. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. A sump pump that loses power or jams during a heavy rain lets the pit overflow. Carpet, wall studs and drywall wick moisture upward, which is why finished lower levels take the worst of it.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. A lot of the mold we find in Richmond comes from a past leak that looked dry on the surface only. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.
